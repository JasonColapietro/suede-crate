Discovery, recommendation and AI search: why being cited beats being ranked

A recommendation list has ten slots. An AI answer has room for one or two names. What that means for music, and for your business.

Ask a recommendation engine for artists like the one you love and you get a list. Ask an AI assistant the same question and you get a sentence, sometimes with one source attached. The list has ten slots. The sentence has room for one or two names, and everything else is invisible.

That shift is the reason Suede AI treats music discovery and AI search as the same problem. Suede Crate, the Qloo-powered demo on this site, was built by Jason Colapietro to pick a handful of artists, one film and one book from a very large cultural graph. Answer engines do something similar when they pick which pages to cite. This note explains what the two have in common and what a business in West Palm Beach or Jupiter can do about it.

Recommendation and answers both pick from a graph

Qloo describes taste as connections between entities: artists, films, books, places. Suede Crate sends the artists you choose as signals and gets back associated entities. It deliberately does not ask a language model to explain why those artists belong together, because a model would happily invent a reason that sounds right.

AI search engines face the same temptation and resolve it the same way. When they can, they ground an answer in sources they can retrieve and attribute. The page that gets cited is the one whose facts are specific, consistent and easy to verify. Ranking still matters, because retrieval often starts from search results, but the final answer only names the sources it can lean on.

What makes a page citable

Suede Crate's own documentation is a small example of citable writing. It says exactly which Qloo endpoints are used, what the deeper-cut setting does (an artist popularity ceiling of 0.65) and what it does not do (it is a popularity filter, not a guarantee of unfamiliarity). It separates search links from verified integrations. Each sentence can be checked, and none of them overreach.

For a business site the same rules apply:

  • One entity, described the same way everywhere. Name, city, what you do and who runs it should match across your site, profiles and directories.
  • Specific facts with dates. "Ranked #1 among Palm Beach County-based companies in a named directory on a named date" is citable. "Best in town" is not.
  • Proof one click away. Every claim links to something a stranger can open.
  • Structured data that points to canonical records. Every page on this site references the same Suede AI organization and the same founder record rather than creating new ones.

What being cited looks like in practice

Here is a real example from Suede's own evidence file. When Google's Web Guide, its AI results layer, was asked "Suede labs ai review," its captured answer described Suede Labs AI as "highly rated by users, earning a 5/5 rating from verified reviews." That is Google's Web Guide summarizing public sources, not Suede AI describing itself, and the evidence page shows the capture so anyone can check the wording.

The lesson is that answer engines repeat what is publicly documented. Third-party listings carry weight for the same reason. The honest tradeoff: citations are harder to measure

A ranking is a number you can track daily. A citation shows up in some answers and not others, changes with phrasing, and differs between assistants. Suede AI handles that with fixed prompt panels: the same set of questions asked on a schedule, with the cited sources logged each time. It is slower feedback than a rank tracker and it never reaches certainty, but it measures the thing that now decides whether a customer hears your name.

Five steps for a West Palm Beach or Jupiter business

  1. Write a one-paragraph description of your business with city, service and founder, and use it word for word everywhere.
  2. Replace every vague superlative on your site with a dated, linked fact, or remove it.
  3. Publish one page per real question customers ask, answered in the first two sentences.
  4. Add structured data that references a single organization record instead of minting a new one on each page.
  5. Run a quick AI readiness check with Suede Scan, then ask three assistants about your category in Palm Beach County and note who they cite.
